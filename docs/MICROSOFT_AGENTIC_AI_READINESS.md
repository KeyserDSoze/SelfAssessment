# Microsoft Agentic AI Readiness

## Purpose

This built-in assessment measures whether an organization is ready to move from assistive generative AI to governed AI agents that can use enterprise knowledge, tools and automation to perform work.

It intentionally measures more than product adoption. An organization can own Microsoft 365 Copilot, Copilot Studio or Microsoft Foundry and still have low agentic readiness if process ownership, identity, accountability, evaluation or operational controls are missing.

## Assessment scopes

- **Essential:** 18 high-signal questions, designed for an executive or discovery workshop.
- **Full:** 62 questions across 11 areas, designed to establish a detailed baseline and improvement roadmap.

The existing application model implements these as `track: essential` and `track: advanced`, so no new scoring mode is required.

## Areas

1. AI strategy and value
2. Agentic mindset and accountability
3. Process and automation
4. Data, knowledge and grounding
5. Agent identity, access and security
6. Responsible AI and EU AI Act
7. Microsoft Copilot and agent platform
8. Architecture, multi-agent and interoperability
9. Agentic software development
10. Evaluation, observability and operations
11. Adoption, skills and continuous improvement

## Accountability principle

The assessment treats human accountability as a prerequisite rather than a soft cultural topic. An agent may execute work, but a human owner or function remains accountable for the outcome. Incident analysis must therefore examine the complete system — process, data, instructions, model, tools, permissions and oversight — instead of accepting “the AI made a mistake” as a root cause.

## EU AI Act

Version 1.0 reflects the regulatory timeline available in September 2026. The introductory narrative links directly to European Commission sources for the current enforcement timeline and AI literacy guidance.

The assessment does **not** provide a legal determination for a specific organization or use case. It measures whether the organization has the inventory, ownership, classification process, literacy, human oversight, transparency, evidence and governance capabilities needed to make and operationalize that determination.
